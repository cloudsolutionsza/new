The Cosmic Apprenticeship

How We Are Formed, What We Inherit, and Where We Are Going

About the book

By the coals of a dying fire, after a family gathering, a son asks his father the question every family eventually asks: “The church says Gogo is in heaven. Uncle says the ancestors watch over us. The pastor says the ancestors are demons. Which one is true?”

His father does not give the answer he was given as a boy. He asks for time. This book is that time.

In The Cosmic Apprenticeship, Siyabonga Mbambo proposes that a human life is an apprenticeship. We arrive unfinished. We are formed by what we inherit and by what we choose. One day we hand on what we have become. We are heirs of the dead, apprentices among the living, and ancestors of the unborn.

Written as a letter to his children and grandchildren, and to readers from Soweto to Seoul, the book takes up the questions that cross every border.

Questions this book takes up

  • What is the “I” that is being formed, and did it exist before we were born?
  • What do we inherit from those before us, and what must we refuse to pass on?
  • How should we honour our ancestors without living in fear of them?
  • What happens when we die, and where are those who never heard the gospel?
  • Is heaven a place above us, or the Source from which everything comes?
  • What are we being prepared for in an age of artificial intelligence, gene editing and a changing climate?

To answer them, Mbambo draws on the Bible, read honestly and in context, and on the African ancestral spirituality he was raised in. He listens to the great traditions of the world: the Yoruba ori, the Hindu ātman, the Buddhist teaching of not-self, Confucian reverence for the generations, and the Jewish chain of transmission. He weighs what science now tells us about the cosmos and our common human origins—naming where the traditions agree and where they differ, plainly and without contempt.

This is not a book that claims to have every answer. It shows its working, including the places where the author has changed his mind. It asks you to argue with it, test it, and go further than he could.

Inside the book

  • Sixteen chapters in seven movements, from the beginning of a life to its commission
  • Practices and questions for reflection at the end of most chapters
  • A Rule of Life for the Apprentice
  • Open questions the book leaves to its readers
  • A glossary, notes and suggestions for further reading
